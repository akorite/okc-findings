## 1. The competition, exactly

Two tracks. Only the **kernel track** matters here; it closes **2026-11-04 23:59 PT = 2026-11-05 07:59 UTC**
(~53 h from the timestamp above). The **serving track** opens Nov 5 and is irrelevant.

**The overall board is the `muse_decode_layer` board, positionally, for ranks 1-505** (verified by comparing
1184 layer rows against 1856 overall rows; the first divergence is overall #506, an entry with no layer row).
`muse_prefill_layer` is excluded from the overall board. So everything below is about one board.

**What `muse_decode_layer` asks for**: one decode step of *one* transformer layer on a single H200, as a
self-contained Python file. The timed call is `kernel_fn(data, state)`; `prepare(weights)` runs once, untimed,
capped at 300 s. The file must build its own CUDA kernels at import/prepare via `torch.utils.cpp_extension.load_inline`
(there is no separate build step) and may do arbitrary lossless weight repacking in `prepare`.

Per layer row: qkv GEMM → paged FP8 GQA attention (page_size 1, either a 2,048-key sliding window or the full
~80k context; 16 query heads, 2 KV heads, head_dim 64) → o GEMM → norm/residual boundary → pooler+router
(top-8 of 256 experts, 32 local under EP8) → W4A16 NVFP4 MoE (SwiGLU, per-expert RMSNorm, weighted sum) →
unpooler → norm/residual. Tokens per step T ∈ {1, 8, 16, 32, 128, 256}.

**Scoring, verified against the judge's own payload**:
- Each of 12 shapes is timed separately: CUDA-graph replay, a 256 MiB cold-L2 read before *every* replay,
  median of paired ratios (each repetition's SGLang time over ours, order-swapped pairs), ~4 µs of replay
  overhead subtracted.
- A load's time = `46·t_sliding + 16·t_full` (the sliding and full shapes at that T).
- `value_us` = **geometric mean of the six load times**; `score = 22620.3 / value_us`.
- Consequence: each of the six loads owns exactly 1/6 of the score *in log space*. 1 µs saved on `decode 1`
  is worth ~5.2× 1 µs saved on `verify 32`.
- **The timed object is ONE layer step**, and L2 is flushed before each replay — so there is *no* cross-step
  weight reuse. The score is single-step bytes over single-step time.

**Rules that constrain solutions**: NVFP4 weights stay NVFP4 and BF16 stays BF16 "or smaller, losslessly";
queries capped at FP8 E4M3, KV at FP8; no key/weight skipping; no caching outputs; no work outside the timed
calls; no tampering with timing or the harness. Final positions are provisional and re-run by humans on held-out
data, so per-shape overfitting and noise farming are discouraged.

## 5. Everything falsified, and why (so a second opinion does not re-propose it)

**MoE (47-57% of the score).** The kernel is a persistent TMA-ring design: 3×36 KB weight slots, 4 consumer
warpgroups over 256-row units, wgmma RS-form, 16 stages/unit, stream-K with per-stage cost tables, one CTA/SM
at 231 KB smem / 96 regs / 576 threads. Measured stream phase **3.87-4.05 TB/s against an independently measured
1-D bulk-copy ceiling of 4.287 TB/s (89.3% of HBM peak)**. Falsified: byte duplication (it reads each touched
expert exactly once — Σjobs/Σdistinct = 1.00-1.06); TMA multicast (every unit has one consumer); ring depth
(4th slot ±1 µs; `NS=4` −0.6%); per-unit CTA-barrier removal (SYNC0 −1.85%); warp-parallel prologue (illegal
address); unit-end dispersion (~1 µs); dropping the split-read-back fence (+0.234%, no row beat A/A); L2 bulk
prefetch (−9.5%); 2 CTAs/SM (register budget); megakernel/persistent-layer (cross-SM hops 3-6× costlier under
HBM saturation); Stream-K as a utilization fix (132/132 CTAs already busy); dequant-to-BF16 + DeepGEMM (loses
at these sizes); Blackwell tcgen05/mxf4 (sm_100 only); Marlin replacements at 16-32 tokens (10-29 µs slower);
NVFP4 code compression (codes are exactly 4.000 bits, iid); the DF data-flag at T≥17 (the payload is not even
in the translation unit for the WGS builds).

**Dense GEMMs (32% of the score).** 5 strictly-serialized cells, 142 MB of 12-bit losslessly packed weights,
2.3-3.6 TB/s. Falsified *on the bf16 board, which times the same five cells at the same m with its own quota*:
0 of 20 geometry routes adopted — more concurrency costs qkv 96→144 CTAs **+35%**, pooler 128→256 **+48%**,
o 256→512 **+25%**; the ramp saturates near one wave, and cuBLASLt/`rq` already win every m≥128 cell by 14-37%.
Also falsified: relaxing the dense bake margins (no margin is mis-set; one is force-overridden anyway), and the
pooler 12-bit pack (slower *and* numerically mismatched: fused 26.6 µs vs packed 30.5 at T=128).

**Attention (15.2%).** Full-attention cells read 655 MB in ~184 µs = **3.5 TB/s**; the judge's SoL implies
4.8-5.2, and the top teams' implied rate is **4.0-4.3**. Sliding cells are latency-bound: 8.42 µs at B=1 for
0.52 MB. Falsified: the launch-plan neighbourhood (0/12 shapes re-planned in a prepare-time sweep of
n_splits/nc/coop), the `FINWARM`/`FLATCOOP1C` dual build (0/12 picks), `SEGK` up (worse) and the merge lever
in three measured ways (11/11 wave-balancing arms lost; single-CTA merges of 128-318 splits −60/−96 µs; cluster
merge −1.1 µs; same-address `red.v4.f32` serialises ~17 ns/op). `EXH8` (f16 exponentials) has no
implementation in our copy and disables PK2. Page-run coalescing is impossible: the judge draws page ids by
`torch.randperm`. TMA `tile::gather4` is sm_100+. A second combine kernel costs ≥1.5-2 µs in-chain.

**Cross-cutting.** L2 persisting carve-out (no intra-step weight reuse; a replay is one step with a cold L2);
TMA tile/2-D mode (same per-SM copy engine, measured parity); graph-level PDL (identical to launch attributes:
1.637 vs 1.641 ms/iter over 100 pairs); code warming (+0.16%); precision trading below the reference (illegal);
routing-dependent prepare-time tournaments (they lost twice because synthetic routing is not the judge's).

**Bottom line**: the two wins we banked came from (a) flipping a vendored build flag and (b) re-tuning a

gap to top-20 is spread thinly across three families that are each near their floor. **We do not have a
mechanism worth +4.4%; we have several worth 0.3-1.5% and a row that is already 1.5σ lucky.**
